# Classroom walkthrough — approximately 15 minutes

This is a walkthrough of the saved September 12, 2026 record, not a live search. Use manual stage navigation; automatic playback only cycles through saved outputs.

## 1. From news to a research puzzle (1 minute)

Open **News Radar**. Explain that a newsworthy development is not automatically a research question. The workflow moves from observable changes to competing mechanisms, literature comparisons, criticism, and a conditional decision.

## 2. Inspect the evidence (3 minutes)

The watchlist contains 11 company or regulatory items with dates, summaries, links, and research signals. Briefly scan commerce protocols, agent payments, pricing policy, and transparency.

Focus on Amazon and Shopify. Avoid labeling one closed and the other open: both combine in-house capabilities, external access, and permissions. Ask which institutional differences need explanation.

Remind students that this is an archived record; the translation did not re-audit the linked claims.

## 3. State the three-regime question (1 minute)

When should a platform choose:
- **In-house provision:** platform control over agents, data, and permissions?
- **Open access:** eligible third-party developers can enter?
- **Selective access:** certification, tiered permissions, and auditing?

## 4. Compare mechanisms (2 minutes)

Open **Mechanisms**. These are candidate explanations, not proven results.

Data advantages and complementary-service profits may favor internal provision. Third-party innovation and merchant heterogeneity may favor openness. Valuable innovation combined with execution risk may favor selective access.

Ask what observation would distinguish each explanation from its alternative.

## 5. Map the trade-offs (1 minute)

Open **Research Map**. Group the six mechanisms into control/internalization, innovation/ecosystem scale, and risk/reliable execution.

Selective access is a regime with its own instruments, not merely an imprecise midpoint.

## 6. Check related work (2 minutes)

Open **Literature**. Pricing algorithms, platform governance, and algorithmic advice already have close precedents. Explain why a generic price-convergence question was dropped.

The three-regime choice is a provisional opportunity. A limited search cannot establish that nobody has studied it.

## 7. Try to reject the idea (2 minutes)

Open **Peer Review**. Discuss multidimensional governance, endogenous institutional choice, bundled policy changes, and unobserved agent behavior.

Cross-platform news provides motivation, not causal identification. Safety is a genuine alternative mechanism, not an explanation to rule out by assumption.

## 8. Rank and revise (3 minutes)

Open **Idea Ranking**, then **Idea Card**.

The proposed model has platform governance choice, developer entry and innovation, then merchant adoption and actions. Its predictions have not yet been derived.

The next decision is whether a minimal model can generate distinct regimes. A controlled experiment could later test objective conflicts and permission design.

End with no-go conditions: no distinct regimes, no new delegation/execution mechanism, or a close existing paper that already answers the question.
